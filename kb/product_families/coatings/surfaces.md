# Afterfade Pro-Tech+ Surfaces

## Surfaces Afterfade sells Pro-Tech+ for
These are the surfaces Afterfade markets and the ones to recommend first: aluminium and PVC window frames, front and exterior doors including composite doors and glass-wall or glazed-door frames (the frame profile only, never the glass pane itself), garage doors, driveway gates and railings, and roller shutters. Powder-coated and painted aluminium are included. This is the core use case and the safest recommendation.

## Other surfaces Pro-Tech+ can be used on
Pro-Tech+ is a multi-surface restorer for interior and exterior use. Beyond the core list above, it can also be used on plastic, painted surfaces, metal, painted wood, and stone — for example garden furniture, black plastics and trim on cars, mirror housings, planters, bins and plastic garden items, painted render walls, and painted (never bare or untreated) wood furniture, doors and shutters.

If a customer asks about one of these, do not say it is out of scope. Say Pro-Tech+ can be used on the material, that Afterfade's own testing and guidance covers aluminium and PVC, and that they should do a test patch on a small hidden area first. Do not promise a specific result or a specific lifespan on these surfaces. For wood specifically: only painted or previously coated wood is suitable — never recommend it for bare or untreated wood.

## Surfaces Afterfade Pro-Tech+ must not be used on
- Anything in permanent or prolonged contact with water, for example pond surrounds, gutters that hold water, or anything below a waterline.
- Floors, decking, steps, paths, or any surface people walk on. If the product is not wiped off thoroughly it becomes slippery when wet, so Afterfade does not recommend it for walking surfaces at all.
- Glass. Treat it as out of scope.
- Bare or untreated wood. Only painted or previously coated wood is suitable.
- Any surface that has not been cleaned, degreased and dried first.

## The test patch rule for Afterfade Pro-Tech+
Always do a test patch on a small hidden area first, before every job, to confirm the effect on that particular material. Always mention this for anything outside aluminium and PVC — including painted wood — and mention it for unusual or valuable items even within the core range.

## Scope of the Afterfade surface list
The working scope is simple: recommend aluminium and PVC first. Plastic, painted surfaces, metal, painted wood and stone can also be used, but sit outside our core tested range. Bare or untreated wood is never suitable. Require a test patch for everything outside aluminium and PVC, and make no written guarantees about surfaces outside aluminium and PVC.
